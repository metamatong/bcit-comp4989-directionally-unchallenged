# Device location and walking

GPS is required when a visitor taps **Get Directions**. The browser requests a fresh location reading immediately from that tap, before photo processing. If photo processing takes long enough for the reading to age, the app refreshes it before matching. On the device, the app intersects its reported accuracy area with the official campus building footprints. Both extraction and metadata-only location requests send matching building IDs, accuracy, capture time and source hash; raw coordinates stay in browser memory. Only matching building IDs are forwarded to the photo provider as context, never raw coordinates or reading metadata. If the browser cannot provide GPS, the route request stops with an error.

The app combines that building match with the photo labels and automatically selects the strongest source-linked starting estimate. GPS narrows the building; it cannot identify the floor. The photo evidence identifies the floor and nearby place. Visitors are not asked to choose a starting room or doorway.

## Guided directions

A complete walking route opens on its first step and starts a foreground GPS watch automatically. The step is visible while the watch waits for a fresh reading. The route step selects the floor map, and the visitor advances with the route controls. The position dot and uncertainty area update from fresh GPS readings when the floor has a geographic alignment. Tracking pauses when the page is hidden and automatically resets and restarts when the visitor returns (unless location permission was denied or revoked). Tracking stops when the visitor opens the route overview, finishes, or leaves the page. **Back to steps** returns to the current step and starts a new watch.

For same-plan indoor corridor steps on geographically aligned pilot floors, the browser prefetches and caches the route's public corridor/lobby walking cells (`GET /api/plans/:planId/walking-surface`, kept separate from the geographic calibration catalogue) and runs client-side corridor map-matching and transient Pedestrian Dead Reckoning (PDR) fusion (`src/location/map-matching.ts`, `src/location/pdr-motion.ts`, `src/location/walkable-grid.ts`, `src/location/pdr-tracker.ts`):

- Readings with reported horizontal accuracy wider than **35 m** are rejected indoors. Rejected fixes at **150 m** or more prompt the visitor to check **Precise Location**. After an aligned same-floor corridor match, `low-accuracy`, `too-far` and `outside-page` rejections hide the live dot on **three or more distinct fixes spanning at least 6 s**, with **at most 4 s** between original capture timestamps. A valid fix or tracking reset clears the streak. A gap longer than 4 s starts a new streak. Once hidden by rejections, the live dot needs a valid fix to return. Outdoor steps, transfers, raw GPS fallback and `outside-building` results use the three-rejection count without a minimum span. Hold expiry applies independently of the rejection count.
- During active indoor corridor tracking, if GPS misses the walking surface, reports weak accuracy, or stops updating, the map can then show a hollow **Last known location** marker at the latest accepted corridor fix. Its age is measured from that fix's capture time. A new match restores the live dot. Before the first corridor match, the app waits for a fix. A floor or transfer change, a revised calibration or walking surface, or pausing or leaving guidance clears the reference.
- The browser uses the snap limit `min(24 m, max(reportedAccuracy, 16 m))` when the accuracy circle overlaps the active building's source footprint. When the circle misses or the footprint is unavailable, the original limit `min(20 m, max(reportedAccuracy, 12 m))` applies. Candidate points come from verified public walking cells and are scored by distance, continuity and a mild, capped preference for the active corridor step.
- **Browser-only motion fusion (3-tier automatic fallback):**
  - **Tier 1 (GPS-only):** Used whenever motion sensors are unsupported, denied, silent (`> 1.5 s` without samples), on desktop browsers, or during non-corridor/transfer steps (`stairs`, `elevator`, `building-link`, `outdoor`).
  - **Tier 2 (Stage 1)** classifies activity as moving, stationary, or unknown. An uninterrupted stationary hold lasts up to **45 s** from the last accepted fix without expanding the uncertainty area. A detected step, movement onset, sensor silence, or floor/transfer transition releases the anchor. To establish a new anchor, the visitor must be stationary and have an accepted corridor fix at most **8 s** old, captured after the applicable step, movement onset or sensor recovery. Relocation while stationary requires three consecutive eligible fixes with distinct original timestamps whose corridor matches are more than 12 m from the anchor and within 5 m of each other, with no more than 10 seconds between fixes. During motion, detected step lengths inform the continuity score.
  - **Tier 3 (Stage 2)** uses a 120-particle filter constrained to the route graph's walking-cell rules. It uses gravity-projected gyroscope yaw, accounts for phone-only turns and suppresses step-only tracking when direction is ambiguous. The live window uses the last accepted GPS capture time. The bridge ends 15 seconds after that capture or on the 20th unanchored step, whichever comes first. A verified stationary hold follows the same anchor rules as Tier 2. The same indoor rejection streak rule applies to the bridge. A gyroscope gap longer than 500 ms or a detected step without usable rotation data also ends the bridge and clears held pre-walk anchors. Particle tracking resumes with an eligible fix captured after a new gyroscope stream begins. `PARTICLE_PDR_ENABLED` is enabled by default; devices without a gyroscope or motion permission automatically fall back to Tier 2 or Tier 1, while physical Android Chrome and iOS Safari verification of gyroscope axes, polarity, and indoor accuracy remains pending.
- **Floor and tracking transitions** reset the tracker when the route enters or leaves a transfer, changes floors, or resumes from the background. Delayed fixes captured before or at the transition cannot seed the new state. If a raw GPS dot appeared while waiting for the walking surface, the first corridor estimate requires a fix captured after the surface loaded. Ordinary same-floor corridor advances preserve tracking. Each unique GPS timestamp is processed once.
- **iOS motion & orientation permission:** **Get Directions** stays dedicated to the required GPS request so optional motion prompts never threaten its 15-second acquisition deadline. On Android Chrome, `devicemotion` and `deviceorientationabsolute` start automatically on Step 1. On iOS 13+ Safari (`DeviceMotionEvent.requestPermission` / `DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission`), aligned guided steps display a compact **Enable compass** pill while orientation permission is still unprompted (and tapping the Step 1 map area, **Next**, **Back**, or **Back to steps** also triggers it). Both permission APIs are invoked synchronously within the same tap so WebKit preserves user activation, mounted trackers subscribe to settlement across Overview/Step transitions, and the app falls back silently to Tier 1 without a heading cone if denied.
- **Live directional heading cone** appears above the uncertainty area and below the center of the live dot on a geographically aligned floor, spanning the full uncertainty ring with a highlighted perimeter arc and radial edges. The browser uses iOS Safari compass readings (accepting reported accuracies up to 75° across 50°–110° cone spreads and rejecting uncalibrated negative readings) or Android Chrome absolute orientation. The Android calculation levels the screen’s right axis to get a heading across flat and upright holds. It hides the cone when that axis is nearly vertical. The browser applies the pilot’s magnetic declination (+15.3° East) and the current floor calibration. A valid compass bearing stays visible when orientation events are quiet. On same-plan corridor steps, a live gyro can refine the estimate and follow a detected turn. Once an unconfirmed turn reaches 3°, the cone clears if the compass has not updated within 3 seconds or the gyro goes silent for over 500 ms. Invalid compass data and floor, calibration, or tracking resets also clear the bearing. The cone needs a valid compass reading to return. On compass-only devices, silence can also indicate a sensor that stopped reporting. Indoor interference and physical phone heading accuracy remain unmeasured.
- **Outdoor steps** are exempt from indoor corridor snapping and motion prediction, retaining standard footprint-overlap and page-bounds checks. When a floor walking surface is unavailable or still loading, the tracker falls back to raw footprint-overlap gating without seeding indoor particles from raw readings.
- The zoomed step map frames strictly around the route step's markers and path (`src/components/RouteMap.tsx`) so live GPS drift does not jump or zoom out the viewport.

The browser requests high accuracy and zero cached age. One-shot photo acquisition (`once()` / `gpsBuildingHint()`) requires a fresh reading (`LOCATION_FRESH_MS = 2000`), with one retry within the original 15-second deadline for a stale or invalid first fix. Permission denial and cancellation end acquisition. Live guided watches accept fixes up to **8 s** old at receipt and clamp up to **1 s** of forward clock skew for age and transition checks. The watch uses the original capture timestamp to deduplicate fixes, including across quiet restarts after a timeout or **15 s** of silence. A GPS-only hold dims the dot, expands the uncertainty ring at `1.5 m/s` past 2 s, and expires **8 s** after capture. The live corridor status announces `Approx. location` and separately displays the map halo's radius in metres rounded up. The raw GPS status shows phone-reported accuracy. Browsers may combine GPS with network signals, and reported accuracy can understate indoor error. Phone use requires HTTPS and location permission. Localhost works on the computer running the app. [W3C Geolocation](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/)

## Reproduce the catalogue

For a direct alignment, the evidence records the BCIT polygon vertex and the matching intersection of two floor-plan line segments, or the pixel-read corner converted to PDF page points. For a stacked alignment, it identifies the same stair, shaft or structural corner on two drawings. The source floor must have an active alignment. The target inherits its geographic uncertainty.

The compiler excludes the last correspondence from the fit. It checks that each fitted point differs by at most 1.5 m and the held-out point by at most 2 m. For a stacked alignment, these limits measure the match between drawings at the source floor's scale. The source floor's own GIS error can also affect the target. SE12 floor 3 retains the original four control positions and roughly 1.05 m check. Phone accuracy remains unmeasured across all floors.

```bash
node --import tsx scripts/prepare-geolocation.ts
npm run data:validate
```

Data validation reproduces the visitor catalogue from the archived source references. The earlier `calibration-candidates.json` records are historical drafts. The compiler uses only the reviewed floor evidence and the archived official footprint, and preserves the original PDF, geometry and geographic-source hashes.

## Privacy and remaining gaps

GPS readings, compass readings and all browser motion/PDR streams (`DeviceMotionEvent`, step counts, anchors, and particle clouds) stay strictly in active browser memory and are omitted from provider prompts, API requests, logs, and persistent session state. The app sends only the building candidates derived on-device. Reloading clears all transient readings and motion state. The browser and operating system may use their own location service.

Offline synthetic simulations cover signal processing, grid connectivity, and comparative tracking behavior. On-campus checks in Android Chrome and iOS Safari still need to verify gyroscope axes and polarity and measure corridor-level error, drift, and recovery. Include slow walking and smooth movement with few detectable steps, such as wheelchair travel. Compare the time it takes the stationary hold to release against the GPS-only corridor matcher using the same route and checkpoints. Guided route progress selects the displayed floor.
